About the dream

This organization didn't start with a plan. It started with a question.

When my son was diagnosed — first with ADHD, then Autism, and more — I threw myself into learning everything I could. Doctors, therapists, forms, evaluations, more forms. I became fluent in a world I never expected to enter. But somewhere in that process, something unexpected happened: I started recognizing myself in his answers.

Watching him get re-tested this year, sitting through the same questions I'd answered on his behalf a hundred times, I realized I wasn't just his advocate. I was looking at my own life through a lens I'd never had access to. So I asked the question a lot of late-diagnosed adults eventually ask: can adults even get diagnosed for this?

A few months and a few rabbit holes later, I had my answer — and an official autism diagnosis of my own. It didn't come with a manual. It came with validation, yes, but also with a sudden, bright light on struggles I'd spent a lifetime quietly working around, never knowing why they were harder for me than for everyone else.

That's the gap this organization exists to close.

There's a lot of support built for catching autism early in children. There's very little built for adults who slipped through — who spent decades adapting, coping, and figuring it out alone, only to find the label later in life, often through their own kids. Getting the diagnosis is just the beginning. What comes next — the resources, the community, the "okay, now what?" — is where most late-diagnosed adults are left to fend for themselves.

I don't want this to be a one-and-done moment for me, and I don't want it to be one for anyone else who finds themselves here. I want the tools and answers I'm gathering for my own path to be out there, organized, and easy to find for the next adult who just had their world turned upside down and doesn't know where to start.

We're in the earliest stage right now — building the foundation, raising seed funds through handmade goods — before we officially file and launch our full community programs. But the mission is already clear, because it's already personal.
